# A fourfold membrane continuum with full-ball tangents

20 November 2026. Candidate developed in this investigation, not a historical
priority claim. Its construction uses standard contraction IFS machinery.

The useful distinction is rigorous: this is a connected, topologically
two-dimensional spatial set with zero volume. It contains genuine planar
square disks in a dense set of orientations in every relative neighbourhood.
At a dense set of points, **every** sufficiently deep normalized view tends
to the entire closed unit ball, not merely along a selected subsequence.

## Exact maps

Let S=[−1,1]²×{0}, and for a,b∈{−1,1} put

    f_ab(x) = x/2 + (a/2,b/2,0).

Let Z rotate about the z axis by π/2. Define

    Q = (1/125) [[53, −60, 96],
                 [60, −75, −80],
                 [96, 80, −3]],
    Q_k = Z^k Q Z^(−k),
    h_k(x) = q Q_k x,          k=0,1,2,3.

Q is the proper rotation with cos θ=−3/5, sin θ=4/5 and axis
(4/5,0,3/5). Every coefficient is rational when q is rational. For the
reference member q=1/4. The family may use any 0<q<1/2.

K_q is the unique compact attractor of these eight contracting similarities.
Every finite word is an exact smaller copy of the whole, and the word-copy
diameters tend to zero along every infinite address.

## Connectedness and retained squares

The four f maps tile S exactly, so S is their subsystem attractor and S⊂K_q.
All four h_k(S) contain the origin, which belongs to S. Therefore the union
of the eight images of any connected set containing S is connected and
again contains S.

The ball C=B(0,√2) contains S and is invariant: |f_ab(x)|≤|x|/2+1/√2≤√2;
|h_k(x)|≤q√2≤√2. Its nested Hutchinson iterates are compact and connected
by the preceding observation, so their intersection K_q is connected.
In particular all f_w(S) are literally contained planar squares. Their union
is dense in K_q because a point of S can be carried along each address.
The rendered panels are these genuine subsets, not artificial surface envelopes.

In fact K_q is path connected. For each of the eight maps F_i, the point
F_i(0) belongs to S, so it can be joined to 0 by a line segment in S.
For an address i_1i_2…, join successive points
F_(i_1…i_n)(0) and F_(i_1…i_(n+1))(0) by the corresponding image of
one such segment. Concatenating these paths on successively shorter time
intervals extends continuously to the addressed limit point, since their
diameters decrease geometrically. Every attractor point is thus joined to 0.

## Symmetry and nonplanarity

Conjugation by Z permutes f_ab and sends h_k to h_(k+1 mod 4). Uniqueness
of the attractor gives Z(K_q)=K_q, hence exact fourfold rotational symmetry.
This establishes the stated symmetry; it does not exclude additional
set symmetries arising from another presentation.

S lies in K_q and h_0(S) lies in a different plane, so K_q has affine span R³.

## Dimension and volume

S⊂K_q gives dim_H K_q≥2. The usual covering argument gives dim_H K_q≤s_q,
where s_q is the unique solution of

    4·2^(−s_q) + 4·q^(s_q) = 1.

For 0<q<1/2, the left side at s=3 is 1/2+4q³<1, so s_q<3. Thus
K_q has zero three-dimensional Lebesgue measure. As q↓0 the upper bound
s_q↓2, allowing an arbitrarily small Hausdorff-to-topological dimension gap.

At q=1/4,

    2 ≤ dim_H K ≤ 1+log_2(1+√2) = 2.271553303163612… < 3.

The Hausdorff dimension is not claimed equal to this upper bound.
The standard inequality dim_top X≤dim_H X and the fact that topological
dimension is integer-valued give dim_top K≤2. The retained square gives
dim_top K≥2, hence **dim_top K=2**. No theorem claiming that zero volume
alone forces a topological-dimension drop is needed.

## Dense rotations, with an elementary proof

The angle θ is an irrational multiple of π: if θ/π were rational,
2cos θ would be both a rational number and an algebraic integer, hence an
integer; here 2cos θ=−6/5. Consequently powers of Q are dense in the circle
group of rotations about a=(4/5,0,3/5).

Let H be the closure of the semigroup generated by Q_0,…,Q_3. A closed
subsemigroup of a compact group is a subgroup: positive powers of each
element recur arbitrarily close to identity, and thus approximate its inverse.
H contains the full circle group about a and its conjugate by Q_1, the full
circle group about Q_1 a. These axes are nonparallel: the exact integer
check in membrane.py computes a nonzero cross product before division.
The infinitesimal rotations about two nonparallel axes, together with their
Lie bracket, span so(3). Hence H is SO(3). Equivalently, the connected closed
subgroup containing both circle groups has full three-dimensional Lie algebra
and therefore equals SO(3). This is the sole short Lie-group step.

Therefore the normals Q_w e_z of the centred squares h_w(S) are dense
in the unit sphere (normal sign is immaterial for a plane).

Every nonempty relatively open subset U of K_q contains an entire cylinder
F_v(K_q): select any addressed point of U and take its prefix long enough
that the cylinder diameter fits inside U. The contained squares
F_v(h_w(S)) then have normals R_v Q_w e_z, again dense. This proves the
claim about every relative neighbourhood, rather than only the origin.

## Every-scale full-ball tangent at the common centre

Write B for the closed Euclidean unit ball and

    A_t = (K_q/t) ∩ B,                 t>0.

We claim d_H(A_t,B)→0 as t↓0. This is Hausdorff convergence of bounded
sets, stronger than merely saying that a contingent/tangent cone is R³.

For each word w of length m using only h maps, K_q contains the centred
planar unit disk scaled by q^m and rotated by Q_w. Fix ε>0. Density of
Q_w e_z and compactness give finitely many words w_1,…,w_N whose unit
disks are collectively ε-dense in B: every x∈B has distance at most ε
from one of these disks. To see this, choose a plane through x and the
origin and approximate its normal; orthogonal projection into a nearby
plane has norm at most |x|≤1 and displacement tending to zero.

Put δ=min_j q^|w_j|>0. For every 0<t≤δ, A_t contains all these rotated
unit disks. Thus sup_(x∈B) dist(x,A_t)≤ε. The reverse Hausdorff distance
is zero because A_t⊂B. This proves convergence for **all** sufficiently
small t, with no subsequence choice.

For a general cylinder similarity F(x)=rRx+p, the point p=F(0) belongs
to K_q and F(K_q)⊂K_q. Therefore

    ((K_q−p)/t)∩B ⊃ R[(K_q/(t/r))∩B],

which has the same full-ball limit. The set of cylinder centres F(0) is
dense in K_q, so full-ball tangent points form a dense countable subset
(possibly a proper subset of all such points).

## Assouad dimension and separation obstruction

The full-ball tangent proves dim_A K_q=3: for every arbitrarily fine finite
grid in B, all sufficiently deep normalized copies contain a separated
point close to each grid point. The uniform local-covering bound in the
definition of Assouad dimension therefore cannot hold with any exponent<3.
The ambient upper bound supplies the reverse inequality.

The presented IFS cannot satisfy the open set condition. If a feasible
nonempty open set O existed, the four h maps would yield 4^m disjoint
images of any ball B(x,ε)⊂O. These images have radius q^m ε and are all
contained in B(0,q^m(|x|+ε)). Cancelling volumes forces

    4^m ≤ ((|x|+ε)/ε)^3

for every m, an impossibility. Coincident maps, if present, would already
violate the disjointness requirement; the argument does not assume that
all finite word maps are distinct.

Because dim_H K_q<3=dim_A K_q, the standard OSC equality of Hausdorff
and Assouad dimensions also excludes **any** alternative finite similarity
presentation satisfying OSC. Historical novelty remains unresolved.

## Scope and next steps

The dense-sheet construction is an explicit homogeneous encoding of a
condensation-set idea: the square is retained by its own tiling subsystem.
That broad technique is existing mathematics. The stronger target for a
literature comparison is the combination of exact finite rational IFS,
fourfold symmetry, topological dimension two, Hausdorff dimension
arbitrarily close to two, and full-ball Hausdorff tangents at a dense set
of points at every small scale.

The prior-art audit identified a directly relevant existing framework:
[Guido and Isola, Tangential dimensions I, Theorem 2.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0305091)
describes tangent spaces at fixed points through expanding inverse copies
of self-similar sets. The full-ball phenomenon should therefore be presented
as an explicit instance of established tangent-space theory, not as a newly
discovered category of tangent behaviour. The audit did not locate this
particular rational fourfold sheet recipe; that absence is not a novelty proof.

The finite images display the sheet subset. They do not resolve the residual
set, prove a dimension value, or prove historical originality. The source
verifier rechecks matrix arithmetic and symmetry; the analytic argument
above is not a proof-assistant formalization or independent review.
